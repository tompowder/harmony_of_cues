Top-Down ASCII RPG
1. Project Overview

Remake the first program I made using Laravel.
The game is a top-down RPG with multiple ASCII-based maps. The player explores the maps, encounters monsters, finds shops and information points, saves and heals at save points, and eventually fights bosses.
The main focus of the game is the turn-based combat system, especially:

A maximum of 4 moves per hero
Choosing moves for multiple heroes
Move synergies
Hero customization
Equipment
Monster abilities
Unique boss AI
Boss equipment and equipment synergies

The player explores the world, learns about enemies and synergies, builds their heroes, and uses that knowledge to overcome increasingly difficult encounters.

2. Core Gameplay Loop

The basic gameplay loop is:

Explore Map
     ↓
Find Encounters
     ↓
Fight Monsters
     ↓
Gain XP / Gold / Equipment
     ↓
Level Up / Learn Moves
     ↓
Visit Shops / Recruit Heroes
     ↓
Learn Monster / Synergy Information
     ↓
Prepare for Boss
     ↓
Defeat Boss
     ↓
Reach Next Map

4. Encounter Points

When the player steps onto an encounter point (.), an encounter can be triggered.
The game selects monsters from the encounter table for the current map.
Each monster has an Encounter Chance that determines how likely it is to appear.

An encounter can contain:

One monster
Multiple monsters

The number and combination of monsters can vary.

For example:

Forest Map

Forest Goblin       40%
green slime         30%
Forest Wolf         20%
Forest Wolf X2      10%

A later map could have a completely different encounter table.

6. Information Points


Information Points (I) provide Wild Information about the area.

There are two levels of information in the game:

Wild Information is obtained from Information Points.

Wild Information is:

Free
Cryptic
Short
Intended to give the player hints
Available before the player has defeated the relevant monster
Not intended to reveal exact statistics or complete mechanics

Wild Information can give clues about:

Monsters found in the area
Monster strengths
Monster weaknesses
Monster abilities
Monster behavior
Bosses
Boss mechanics
Synergies
Other useful gameplay information

For example:

------------------------------------
| There is a rare chance to find a |
| Golden Goblin in these woods.    |
| It will flee after some time,    |
| but if you defeat it in time,    |
| you will be richly rewarded.     |
------------------------------------

This tells the player that:

A Golden Goblin can appear in the area.
It is rare.
It can flee.
There is a reward for defeating it quickly.

The purpose of Wild Information is to let the player learn through hints and observation without giving them the complete answer.


Pristine Information is the detailed version of information.
It is normally obtained by purchasing information from a Shop and rearly from Information Points.

Pristine Information can provide:

Exact or detailed statistics
Abilities
Move information
Affinity
Encounter Chance
Gold reward
XP reward
Drop information
Detailed descriptions
Detailed mechanics
Synergy combinations
Other information that would allow the player to fully understand how something works

Monster Pristine Information has one important requirement:
The player must defeat the monster at least once before its Pristine Information can be purchased.

This creates a progression where the player first gets a hint, then experiences the enemy themselves, and finally has the option to purchase a complete explanation.

10. Weapons

A hero and monster can have a weapon.

A weapon can:

Increase stats
Give a move

Normally, an entity can only equip one weapon unless an ability allows multiple weapons.

Weapon Moves

Moves gained from weapons are temporary.
If the weapon is removed, the move is removed.

For example:

Flame Sword

Attack +5

Move:
Flame Slash

Removing the sword removes Flame Slash.

11. Armor

Armor can:

Increase stats
Give an affinity
Give an additional ability

Normally, an entity can equip only one armor unless an ability allows multiple armor pieces.

Heroes can only gain an affinity through armor.

When a hero replaces their armor, the previous armor is moved to the player's Inventory.
Armor dropped by monsters or bosses is also added to the Inventory.

12. Boss Equipment

Bosses can have unique weapons and armor.

A boss weapon or armor can:

Change the boss's AI
Provide special moves
Provide abilities
Enable special synergies


This means boss equipment can be part of the boss's actual combat design.

Boss equipment can potentially be obtained by the player after defeating the boss.
Each boss equipment item can normally only be obtained once.

15. Turn Structure

The player selects actions for the heroes.

For example:

Hero 1:

-----------------
| Slashing 0 hp |
| Tackle   1 hp |
-----------------

Attack -> Slashing -> Monster 1

Hero 2:

--------------------
| Water Blast 3 mp |
| Heal        5 mp |
--------------------

Magic -> Water Blast -> Monster 1

After the heroes have selected their actions, the game checks whether the selected moves create a synergy.

The turn is then resolved.

16. Run

Run is a battle-wide action.

If the player selects Run with a hero, the game stops asking for actions from the remaining heroes.

The party then attempts to escape.

If successful:

Battle ended.
The heroes escaped.

If unsuccessful, the enemy gets its turn and the battle continues.

17. Skip

A hero can choose Skip.

Skip does not perform an attack or magic move.

A hero with 0 HP automatically uses Skip unless an ability specifically allows them to act differently.

Skip can be a part of a move synergy

Weapon Moves

A weapon that provides a move only provides that move while the weapon is equipped by a hero.
A weapon in the Inventory does not provide its move.

25. Synergy Moves

When specific moves are selected by different entities during the same turn, they can combine into a synergy move.

The original moves are not used separately.

Instead, the synergy move replaces them.

For example:

Hero 1 -> Water Blast
Hero 2 -> Heal

can become:

SYNERGY! Holy Water

26. Synergy Effects

Synergies can:

Affect multiple entities
Have other special effects

    Holy Water

27. Synergy Stats

By default, a synergy uses the average stats of the entities involved.

Example:

Hero 1 Magic = 20
Hero 2 Magic = 10

Average Magic = 15

The synergy uses 15 Magic.

However, a synergy can define its own stat calculation.

Possible calculations include:

Average stats
Highest stat
Lowest stat
Added stats
The targets entities stats
Another custom calculation

For example:

This synergy uses the highest Magic stat instead of the average.

28. Hero and Monster Synergies

Synergies are not limited to heroes.

A monster and another monster can have a synergy.

A hero and a monster can potentially have a synergy as well.

This could be used for special mechanics, counters, bosses, or unique encounters.

For example, a boss and a minion might use two moves together to activate a powerful attack.

31. HP

HP means Hit Points.

They automatically use Skip unless an ability allows something different.

32. MP

MP means Magic Points.

MP is used for magic moves.

Monsters can have MP, but monster MP is not displayed in the battle UI.

MP can be recoverd by levling up, Ability and at a save point

33. Attack

Attack determines the strength of attack moves.

Example:

Attack = 10

Tackle:
10 × 1.5 = 15 base damage

The actual damage is then affected by Defense and other effects.

34. Magic

Magic determines the strength of magic moves.

Example:

Magic = 10

Water Blast:
10 × 2.1 = 21 base damage

The actual damage is affected by Defense, affinity, abilities, and other effects.

35. Defense

Defense reduces incoming damage.

Physical attacks use the target's Defense.

Magic moves partially ignore Defense.

For example, if a target has:

Defense: 5

and a physical move deals:

10 damage

the target could take:

10 - 5 = 5 damage

Magic moves can ignore a percentage of Defense.

For example:

Magic ignores 50% of Defense.

Defense = 5
Effective Defense = 2.5

The final damage is rounded down where required.

The exact damage formula can be adjusted during balancing.

Piercing moves ignore all defense

40. Leveling

Every hero and monster type has its own level schema.

The schema defines the entities natural stats at Level 100.

Example:

Level 100

HP:      800
MP:      400
Attack:  300
Magic:   500
Defense: 200


The base stats at other levels can be calculated from the Level 100 values.

Example:

800 / 100 × 25
= 200 HP at Level 25

The system can also support levels above 100.

Example:

800 / 100 × 150
= 1200 HP at Level 150

If a calculation results in a decimal, the value is rounded up where appropriate.

For heroes, Defense does not increase automatically through leveling. 
Defense can instead be increased through Stat Points, armor, and abilities.

48. Bosses

49. Monster AI

Bosses and som monsters can have their own AI rules.

Instead of randomly selecting moves, a monster or boss can react to the battle.

Example boss ai:

If HP > 75%:
    Use normal AI

If HP <= 75%:
    Use aggressive AI

If HP <= 40%:
    Activate special ability
    Use powerful attacks more often

Bosses can also react to the player's actions.

For example:

If player uses Fire:
    Use Fire Counter

If player repeatedly uses Magic:
    Use Magic Shield

If player heals:
    Target the healer

This makes each boss feel different.

54. Combat Design Principles

The combat system should focus on planning and interaction rather than simply increasing numbers.

A player should think:

Which moves should I select?

Which enemy should I target?

Can I create a synergy?

Should I use a powerful move now or save MP?

Should I risk an HP-cost attack?

Should I heal?

Should I try to run?

What does this boss appear to be preparing?

Which hero should receive this equipment?

Which four moves should I keep?

The goal is for decisions to matter.

55. Development Plan

The entire game should not be built at once.

The first prototype should be a small vertical slice.

Prototype
1 map
2 heroes
3 monsters
1 shop
1 information point
4-8 moves
1-2 synergies
1 boss
1 boss equipment synergy

The prototype should be playable from beginning to end.

After testing it, adjust:

Damage formulas
XP
Gold
MP costs
HP costs
Defense
Encounter chances
Synergy power
Boss difficulty
Leveling
Stat points

Only after the core combat is fun should the game be expanded with more maps, heroes, monsters, bosses, and mechanics.

56. Current Design Philosophy

The game should be easy to understand at a basic level but have depth when the player learns the systems.

A new player should understand:

Attack
Magic
Run
Skip

But an experienced player can think about:

Equipment
Abilities
Affinity
Move slots
Stat builds
Synergies
Monster combinations
Boss AI
Boss equipment
Boss synergies

The complexity should come from how the systems interact, rather than from having hundreds of separate mechanics.

57. Core Identity

The game can be summarized as:

A top-down ASCII RPG focused on strategic turn-based combat, limited move sets, customizable heroes, and powerful move synergies.

The most unique parts of the game are:

Four-move limit
Planning moves for multiple heroes
Synergy moves replacing the original moves
Heroes customizable through stat points
Equipment that changes available moves and abilities
Monsters with different behaviors and abilities
Bosses with unique AI
Boss equipment that creates special synergies
Information systems that let players learn about enemies and synergies
ASCII maps that connect exploration and combat
